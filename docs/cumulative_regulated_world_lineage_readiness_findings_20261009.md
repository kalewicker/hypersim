## Versioned ancestry

The new measurement codec distinguishes:

- **Entity:** the gene's persistent lineage identity, copied through actual paid
  births and preserved during in-place retuning.
- **Version:** the particular gene or editor state produced by a native edit.
  Retunes and policy writes receive explicit version identities, including
  neutral writes. Deleted gene versions remain as tombstones.
- **Organization acquisition:** the edit that acquired the gene's reaction stage.
  Neutral retuning preserves it; a neutral duplicate copies it from its source.
  A changed stage receives a new acquisition identity.

Identities are scoped to their source world; measurement records carry that
source explicitly. The sampler includes every retained living or dead cell in
its keyed ranking, then preserves both registered slots at censuses 0,2,4,8,
including empty and outside-horizon slots. Every selected path follows actual
paid births. Each ancestor's history ends at the birth of the next path member.
Nonheritable transmission resets both gene and policy provenance to the founder.

Novelty requires the first postfounder executable stage absent from the founder
and every earlier stage on that path. Reacquiring a lost stage is not a new
innovation. Candidate pairs retain exact acquired identities; transmission
counts require that identity in the copied program. Descendant activity requires
actual metabolic receipts using that acquisition, rather than a different gene
at the same stage. Deletion or stage-changing retuning can remove a candidate;
its event and tombstone history remain recorded.
