## Measurement boundary

The implementation reconstructs program ancestry from graph-derived gene states,
native edit receipts and actual paid births. Every founder gene has a founder
origin. Every appended gene has a cell/receipt origin that is transmitted through
the actual birth mapping. Parent history stops at the birth of the next member on
the selected path; later edits in a parent cannot be attributed to an earlier
child. Nonheritable births reset to the registered founder mapping. Native
metabolic bindings identify which inherited organization was actually used.

Seeded founder machinery, authored probes, external mutation, nonheritable edits
and blind endogenous edits retain different provenance. A new organization is
the first retained executable reaction stage absent from the founder and earlier
retained organization. Neutral duplicates remain recorded but are not a new
organization. All chronological pairs of retained novel organizations are
enumerated; there is no ranking by utility or intervention outcomes.

The current codec only represents adjacent-stage reactions. This is a bounded
measurement of that repertoire, not general program semantics or proof of
minimal/irreducible complexity. Editor regulation is not evaluated here.

## Native intervention assays

The planner fixes the entire request list before new native assay outcomes.
Nineteen distinct role/program requests cover the two authored pairs. Repeated
semantic inputs may share an assay within one role; intact, sham and restoration
roles execute separately even when their programs are identical.

Each assay constructs a matched graph compartment from the selected semantic
program projection: capital 40, environment 512, identical seed, frozen arm,
editor mode zero, four cycles and one paid copy request. This standardizes context
rather than cloning the endpoint's physiological state. Diagnostic branches
never feed back into a source population. The native matcher/apply path executes
all reactions and physical settlement. The score is final parent capital plus
transferred child capital minus initial parent capital; child environmental
credit is excluded. Storage, execution and actual copying costs are included.
Each ancestral before/after comparison also subtracts the two-unit acquisition
cost. Unfunded copy requests retain their full resource horizon and refusal
status; they cannot silently pass a candidate comparison.

Group removal deletes every endpoint copy of the earlier acquired stage while
preserving the remaining order. Inactive padding replaces those copies with
legal nonexecutable stage -1, preserving storage/copy load. A single-copy removal
exposes redundancy. The sham rebuilds the same program; restoration reinserts the
removed group at its original positions. All resulting graphs retain a valid
schema, and their actual native traces are independently checked.

Both authored controls meet the diagnostic functional/dependence and transmission
conditions. Neither counts as autonomous discovery. Removing one redundant copy
actually improves the score by reducing storage and copying costs; removing
the entire stage-1 group destroys the later pathway's benefit. This is why
single-gene essentiality and graph size are inadequate primary criteria.
