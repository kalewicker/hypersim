The raw records retain all claimed-node owners, and exclusive category sets partition the rejected-candidate denominator exactly. Blocking-node incidence additionally counts each bound-variable position; it is not a candidate count. No other-member-0 blocker occurred in these intervals.

Focal losses leave later quarter summaries empty of focal candidate events; those rows remain in the fixed 1,000-step horizon. Active-root exposure removes that denominator ambiguity but does not make the two arms equivalent states. Restored cultures have different graphs, root populations, immutable ancestry, candidate pools and keyed-score assignment by pool position. The observed rates are conditional on those diverged states. Higher restored truncation fractions (40.18% and 58.47%, versus faithful 4.15% and 1.64%) also show that truncation cannot be equated with faithful failure from whole-interval totals alone.

This identifies actual competing winners; it does not establish that removing one category would make the focal candidate win, because another winner may then claim the node.

Canonical truncation removed further terminal candidates. The raw witnesses expose their membership, but the instrument did not submit them or assign counterfactual conflict outcomes. Increasing the cap changes submitted input positions and therefore changes which keyed random draws are assigned to bindings. A later cap assay must distinguish membership changes from that draw-assignment effect.
